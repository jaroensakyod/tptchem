# Product Brief: Lab Safety Task Cards

- **Product ID:** CN-LS-TASK-CARDS
- **Subject/topic:** Chemistry Foundations - laboratory safety decisions
- **Format number/name:** 2 - Print/Digital Task Cards
- **Target grade/course:** Grades 9-11 General/Honors Chemistry
- **Buyer use case:** Bell ringer, partner review, early-finisher practice, substitute activity, or small-group reteach
- **Seasonal window:** Beginning of course; first 1-3 weeks
- **Duration:** 25-45 minutes; optional 50-60 minute rotation
- **Prerequisites:** Students know the local safety contract, can identify basic PPE, and understand that teacher/site policy and current SDS instructions control

## Learning targets

1. Identify the specific evidence that makes a laboratory choice unsafe or incomplete.
2. Select the safest next move from realistic alternatives and distinguish an immediate action from a later cleanup step.
3. Explain or communicate a safety decision using observable language rather than a vague rule.

## Reference-text scope

- **Concepts:** PPE and clothing; labels/SDS awareness; heating; glassware; housekeeping; waste/broken glass; emergency communication; reporting incidents; no unauthorized experiments, food, or drink.
- **Vocabulary:** hazard, risk, exposure, PPE, SDS, wafting, contamination, broken glass, spill, emergency equipment, teacher/site procedure.
- **Difficulty boundary:** One observable decision per card; Core requires a choice plus a short reason; Honors compares competing actions, prioritizes urgency, or identifies a missing fact that must be checked.
- **License/use restriction:** Chem Pride is benchmark-only unless the owner grants permission. OpenStax Chemistry 2e is not used as direct generative-AI input or commercial adaptation without permission. Student-facing wording and visuals must be original and cross-checked against rights-cleared safety authorities.

## Original item plan

The set contains **24 original cards divided into four functional portions**, plus a recording sheet and teacher key. The portions are intentionally different so the product is not Scenario Analysis in a smaller rectangle.

### Portion A - Notice and pause (6 cards)

- **Student work:** Find the one detail that changes the safety decision; mark the first safe pause/stop point.
- **Card types:** evidence hunt, hazard-detail match, incomplete-PPE check, label-detail check.
- **Primary skills:** observation and risk recognition.

### Portion B - Choose the next move (6 cards)

- **Student work:** Select the safest immediate action from three plausible options.
- **Card types:** action choice, sequence the first two moves, safe/unsafe comparison.
- **Primary skills:** procedure choice and harm reduction.

### Portion C - Communicate and follow procedure (6 cards)

- **Student work:** Decide who must be notified, what information to report, or which local source must be checked before continuing.
- **Card types:** report-it, SDS/site-policy check, broken-glass/waste routing, heat/glass response.
- **Primary skills:** communication and procedural judgment.

### Portion D - Transfer and repair misconceptions (6 cards)

- **Student work:** Explain why a tempting action fails, repair an unsafe statement, or compare two cases with one changed detail.
- **Card types:** misconception repair, compare/contrast, prioritize, Honors extension.
- **Primary skills:** transfer, justification, and metacognition.

### Scoring and differentiation

- **Support:** simplified language, category bank, two-option choice, sentence frame, and a clear rights-cleared visual whenever the student must recognize observable evidence.
- **Core:** all four portions; choice plus one-sentence rationale.
- **Honors:** competing-risk cards, missing-information prompts, and policy/SDS justification.
- **Recording sheet:** card ID, evidence, chosen action, reason, and confidence check.
- **Key:** intended answer, acceptable equivalent wording, why distractors fail, misconception tag, and site-policy note.

### Misconceptions to target

- “Be careful” is an observable safety action.
- Cleanup always comes before notifying the teacher.
- A clear/colorless substance is automatically safe.
- A hot plate is safe as soon as the switch is off.
- Broken glass can go into ordinary trash.
- A generic rule overrides current site policy or SDS instructions.
