# US Chemistry Demand Brief - August 2026

## Decision

Build the first product family around **Chemistry Foundations / Unit 0** for grades 9-11: lab safety, laboratory equipment, SI measurement, significant figures, scientific notation, dimensional analysis, and density. Launch **Lab Safety** first.

AP Chemistry begins with atomic structure and should be treated as a separate product line, not mixed into this general/honors sequence.

## What students are studying now

Evidence from current and recent district pacing guides shows a consistent August sequence:

| Source | Opening sequence | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Fulton County Schools, 2026-27 | Aug 3-21: matter, measurement, calculations, chemistry tools; atomic theory follows | Immediate demand for foundations resources |
| Park Hill, 2026 | Safety and SI units; safety one-pager and measurement challenge | Activities should combine safety with measurable performance |
| Northside ISD | Safety and procedures, then introduction to chemistry | Safety is a true beginning-of-course need |
| Southington Grade 11 | Matter and measurement; safety assessment, observation, separation, density | Products need assessment and lab-readiness, not decoration only |
| Marlboro Grade 11 | One-week chemistry skills: conversions, sig figs, percent error, safety, density, temperature | A coherent Unit 0 bundle has strong curriculum fit |
| AACT Quick Start | First ten meetings include safety, SI, measurement, sig figs, scientific notation, dimensional analysis, matter | Supports a multi-product foundations family |

## Reference-text comparison

OpenStax Chemistry 2e Chapter 1 covers chemistry in context, matter classification, physical and chemical properties, measurements, uncertainty/accuracy/precision, and mathematical treatment. It is useful for scope, vocabulary, sequencing, and difficulty benchmarking.

**Commercial-use rule:** the current English Chemistry 2e is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Do not copy, adapt, remix, or closely paraphrase its prose or questions in a paid TPT resource. All commercial product prompts, examples, scenarios, diagrams, and answers must be original.

## Curriculum x textbook x worksheet gap

| Need | District pacing | Textbook support | Common worksheet gap | Product response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lab safety decisions | First days | Limited procedural depth | Recall-only rules | Original scenario analysis and performance assessment |
| Equipment use | First 1-2 weeks | Contextual | Clip-art labeling without use cases | Realistic, licensed equipment visuals plus selection tasks |
| Measurement | First 1-3 weeks | Strong | Repetitive calculations | Reading tools, uncertainty, reasoning, and error analysis |
| Differentiation | General and honors coexist | One reading level | One worksheet for all | Core, scaffold, and honors pathways |
| Assessment | Safety test and unit checks | End-of-section exercises | No retake or data support | A/B forms, retake, keys, and item-analysis sheet |

## Quality and rights rules

1. Use ACS and district guidance as factual authorities; write every student-facing item originally.
2. Never assume a free download is commercially reusable.
3. Record URL, creator, license, allowed use, and access date before any external asset enters a product.
4. Use verified public-domain, CC0, compatible commercial-license, or self-created assets only.
5. Do not use crude hand-drawn lab equipment where identification accuracy matters.
6. Every product must include a complete key, teacher directions, differentiation, and a source/rights page.
7. Stop release for any factual ambiguity, missing answer, unclear image right, clipped page, or answer-key mismatch.
